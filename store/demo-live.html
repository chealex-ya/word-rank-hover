How much English do you actually need?

Learners often worry about vocabulary size, but the numbers tell a comforting story. The most common thousand words in English already cover the vast majority of everyday conversation. Every word beyond that ceiling pays back a little less, so knowing which words to learn next matters more than learning many of them at random.

Consider the word language. It sits comfortably inside the top two thousand — the vocabulary people rely on daily, in meetings, on the street, in every paragraph of this article. If a word lives in this band, you will meet it again soon; learning it is almost never wasted effort.

Now compare a professional label like consultant. It is useful — if you happen to work with one, or happen to be one — but nobody needs it to order coffee. Mid-band words are worth picking up when your life or your job makes them familiar.

And then there is ubiquitous, a beloved word of essayists and almost nobody else. It shows up in careful writing far more often than in speech, and a learner can happily postpone it for years without missing a beat.
